# Variations, documents and wet days

Three records he already keeps, usually on paper or not at all. Each one exists
because of a specific argument it settles later.

---

## Variations — the money one

**Where:** the house page › *Variations* › **Raise one**

Two taps, on purpose.

1. **Save as draft.** Describe the change and put the amount in. Nothing is sent
   and the owner sees nothing. He can do this standing in the room while he is
   still working the price out.
2. **Send.** Now it goes to the owner for approval, and this is the moment it
   becomes an offer.

> **Once it is sent, the amount is locked.** There is no edit. If the price was
> wrong, raise a new variation — which is also how it works on paper. Rewriting
> the number an owner already agreed to would destroy the only thing this record
> is for.

A credit goes in as a **negative** amount. Deleting an item from the contract is
a variation like any other.

### What the owner sees

The variation, the amount on its own line, and two buttons. When they choose,
they are asked for a **six-digit code**, which is emailed to the address they
registered. Typing it back is what puts their name on the decision.

The code is there because the house link is meant to be shared. It goes round
the family WhatsApp group, and it ends up with parents, sometimes a broker. A
plain *Approve* button on a link like that means a $6,000 agreement can be
tapped by somebody who is not paying for the house.

**Be clear about what the code does and does not prove.** It stops a casual tap,
and it ties the decision to a named inbox with a time and an address beside it.
It is not a signature: anyone holding the link can register their own email and
use it. Two things cover that, and they are worth knowing:

- **Everyone registered on the house is emailed the moment a decision is made.**
  So an approval by the wrong person surfaces that hour, not at final invoice.
- **He can see and remove everyone who has registered**, on the house page under
  the link. If a name there is not someone who should be approving spending,
  revoke them.

### Reading it back

The house page shows each variation with who decided and when. The unapproved
ones are also chased in the Friday email, because an unapproved variation is
work that cannot start.

## Documents — the owner's shelf

**Where:** the house page › *Documents*

Plans, permits, the contract, certificates, warranties. Pick a file, give it a
name, tap the category. He can leave the name blank and it uses the filename.

> **Everything here is visible to the owner immediately.** There is no private
> half of this shelf, and that is deliberate — a per-file "internal" switch is
> one mistap away from sending a subcontractor's quote to the client, and the
> mistap happens at 5pm on site.
>
> **Quotes, margins, subbie paperwork and anything with his costs in it do not
> go in here.**

Removing a document deletes the file properly, not just the link. A superseded
plan set should be removed — a stale drawing someone builds from is worse than
no drawing at all.

PDF, JPEG, PNG or WebP, up to 25 MB.

## Wet days — the one that protects the handover date

**Where:** the house page › *Wet days* › **Log a day**

Under a standard HIA or MBA residential contract, days lost to inclement
weather entitle him to an extension of time. In practice that only holds if the
days were recorded **as they happened**. A log written in March covering
November convinces nobody.

Two things to get right:

**Log the day work was lost — not every day it rained.** The contract's test is
lost work, not rainfall. A wet Sunday costs nothing. A wet Tuesday that stops a
pour costs a day, and often the days after it while the site dries out. Those
are all loggable, one per day.

**Say what it stopped.** The common reasons are one tap. A notice that says
"wet" invites an argument; one that says *"slab pour pushed to Thursday, site
too soft for the pump"* usually ends it.

He can log yesterday, or last Wednesday — entries are dated by the day they
apply to, not the day he typed them. Logging the same day twice corrects it
rather than counting it twice.

### Using the log

**Copy the log for a delay notice** puts the whole thing on the clipboard,
formatted for pasting straight into the extension-of-time email he has to send
anyway.

### What the owner sees

The dates and what was lost, and nothing else. No promise that the time has been
absorbed and no claim that the handover date is unaffected — because any such
sentence is one an owner can quote back at him in the dispute these very days
exist to support. The same rule applies to anything he types into an update
himself: **never tell an owner that lost time is "already accounted for" in
their dates.**

If days were lost in the week, the Friday email names them.
